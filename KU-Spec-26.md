# Computer Science Competition

## A Note About Computer Science Projects

Computer Science is a field that sits at the intersection of engineering, science, and art. This competition encourages creative freedom and unique solutions. As such, scoring is subjective and is up to the judges' discretion.

## Introduction

In the royal town of Mautern, where the King and Queen reside, the scholars recently came across a cache of magical orbs that operate much like the smartphones we own. The town's blacksmith, Gunter, has requested your guild's help in developing an application for his blacksmithing shop. His store has been experiencing a lot of difficulty managing incoming orders and he thinks that these orbs could be the key to improving his customers' experience.

*Assume the orbs have the capability of modern-day smartphones (i.e. internet access, satellite capability, gps technology, whatever you can think of…)*

## Competition Overview

Software development is a foundational skill in the field of Computer Science, and this competition puts you on the front lines of development. You are expected to assess the clients' needs, design a solution, and implement it. Through this competition, you will get a hands-on look at the software development life cycle.

In this competition, you will develop and present a blacksmith storefront application optimized for mobile devices (think like the Amazon customer webpage). Team sizes are from 1-5 students. You should come to the competition with an application that meets the Blacksmith's requirements listed below. On the day of the competition, you will be asked to integrate an additional feature into your app within a certain time constraint.

## Design

Using the programming language of your choice (Python, C++, JavaScript, HTML, etc.), design and implement a mobile store user-interface application for Gunter's blacksmith shop. For this competition, it is important to assess the needs of your client to begin the construction of a product. Your product should adhere to the theme of the competition and be designed with customer usability in mind.

### User needs:
- Place an order for any of the tools that Gunter provides:
  - Sword
  - Plate Armor
  - Chain Mail
  - Cooking Pot
- See where their order lies on Gunter's to-do list.

### Additionally:
- Introduce one more feature to help Gunter run an efficient shop.
  - We really encourage creativity here and hope to see your ability to identify a problem and develop a solution to it.
- Your team will be challenged to add an additional feature that will be revealed on the day of competition.
- Your team is also required to utilize a GitHub repository for version control and final submission.
  - A minimum of 3 commits to your repository are required. This can include a commit of the day of challenge, but we highly recommend committing to the repository more often.

## Code Requirements

Your product must meet all the design requirements listed above. In addition, we expect the following with your final submission to your public GitHub repository.

### Project Expectations:
- **README**: This document should detail the functionality and features of your product.
- **Documentation**: All code should be well documented. (DO NOT use AI to write comments.)
- **Code base**: All necessary files to host your project.
- **Submission**: Must submit GitHub link to mollymasalskis@ku.edu before the end of the work time provided. This email should contain an accessible link to your repository, group number as the subject line, and the names of each team member below the link.
  - *Note: Any changes made to the GitHub or project after this cut off will result in disqualification from the competition.*

## Testing

This competition will run in two segments: a challenge and final presentation.

### Challenge (2.5 hours):
- When the students arrive, they will be assigned team numbers and directed to a workspace. Students will then receive information about a surprise addition they will be required to add to their project. They will then be given 2.5 hours to complete this challenge.

### Presentation (45 minutes):
- Afterwards they will be given 45 minutes to practice and prepare a presentation about their project. Each group will present 3 separate times to 3 judges, and their scores will be averaged together to determine their final score. Students will present on their own personal devices. The presentation layout will be different than traditional presentations, where judges will walk around the room and go to various teams at different times in the 45-minute window.

## Presentation

Presentations are where teams will demo and explain their project and the decisions that went into creating it. Students should expect to have their project running on their personal device for a live demonstration and walk through with a judge. Each team will present and demo their project three times each, where they will be evaluated on the rubric below.

### Presentation Expectations:
- **Speech**: Teams will be given 3 minutes to present and 2 minutes to answer questions.
  - Each team member should participate in the presentation.
- **Content**: Teams should walk the judge through their project, performing a live demo and providing an explanation of functions and features. Students should be prepared for questions about their project, how it works, and why they made those decisions.
  - *Note: Students should aim to explain the elements listed on the rubric.*

## Scoring

Teams will be evaluated on the categories stated in the rubric below during their presentation. Documentation will be reviewed separately, outside of the presentation period. Judges will fill out the rubric below and average the 3 evaluations together to create a teams' final score. In the event of a tie, judges will have a private discussion period where they will come to a majority consensus of the winner. Understand that some sections of the rubric are subjective and will be up to judge and HSD organizers' discretion.

## What to Expect Day of Competition

Students are required to bring their own laptops and chargers. Students will also be able to access the KUGUEST Wi-Fi network. Teams will be provided with a team number and directed to a workspace on arrival. Once settled, they will be tasked with the challenge of adding an additional element to their project. This element will be revealed at this time in the competition.

Teams will have 2.5 hours to add to their project and submit their GitHub link, along with team number and name to mollymasalskis@ku.edu. During this time, teams will have access to ask various mentors if help is needed. Mentors will be stationed around the work room. Teams will then have a 30-minute break, followed by a 45-minute presentation block. Presentations will then commence; teams are expected to demo their project for up to 4 minutes and answer questions during a 2-minute questioning period.

After the 45 minutes, the scores will be calculated and the winners will be announced.

## AI Usage Statement

Artificial Intelligence is a powerful tool that many software companies have quickly adopted into their development workflow. Following suit with industry and academic standards, teams are permitted to use artificial intelligence to assist in code development. Teams are required to document all use of AI and are expected to understand and justify all design choices. Failure to document AI usage could lead to immediate disqualification.
